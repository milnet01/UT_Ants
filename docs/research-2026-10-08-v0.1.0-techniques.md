# Research, 2026-10-08: cheap techniques for 0.1.0's open items

> **Purpose — what published work says about the open 0.1.0 items, so each
> is built with the cheapest proven method.** Asked for by the user on
> 2026-10-08. Produced by a research agent searching the web; nothing here
> was measured in this repository.

**How sure each claim is.** **[verified]** was read from the primary source
in that run. **[memory]** is the agent's own knowledge, unchecked. Check a
[memory] claim before building on it.

## UTA-0325 — the cluster light loop, once per wave

Our clusters store an index list per cluster
(`clusterIndices[cluster * CLUSTER_CAPACITY + k]`).

- **Merge sorted lists across the wave [memory].** Keep each cluster's list
  sorted. Each step, `next = subgroupMin(myIndex)` (an exhausted lane holds
  `0xFFFFFFFF`); stop when `next` is that value. Every lane shades `next`,
  so the light's data loads once per wave. Lanes holding `next` advance.
  It costs as many steps as the wave's lists hold distinct lights.
- **Simpler: loop over distinct clusters [verified pattern].**
  `c = subgroupBroadcastFirst(cluster)`; lanes in cluster `c` shade its list
  and leave. Cheap when a wave sits in one cluster; pays once per cluster at
  depth edges.
- **Call of Duty: Infinite Warfare's form (Drobot, SIGGRAPH 2017)
  [verified].** A per-tile light bitmask plus z-binning, OR-merged across
  the wave and walked with `findLSB`. Their numbers: registers per lane 56 to
  32, cost 100% to 72%; one PS4 scene 5.7 ms to 4.6 ms. Needs the light
  list rebuilt as bitmasks.
- **Extensions:** `GL_KHR_shader_subgroup_ballot` and `_arithmetic`. Check
  `VkPhysicalDeviceSubgroupProperties` for the fragment stage and both
  operation bits at start-up, and keep the per-lane loop behind a
  specialization constant as the fallback.
- **Pitfalls.** A lane shades lights from its neighbours' clusters, which is
  invisible only if every light's falloff is exactly zero at its radius.
  Helper lanes join subgroup operations. Confirm scalar loads in the compiled
  shader (`RADV_DEBUG=shaders`): a Mesa bug left uniform-looking loads
  per-lane. Nothing solid was found on lavapipe's subgroup support; query it.

Links: [Drobot 2017](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2017/2017_Sig_Improved_Culling_final.pdf),
[Vulkan subgroups guide](https://docs.vulkan.org/guide/latest/subgroups.html),
[RADV codegen bug](https://bugs.freedesktop.org/show_bug.cgi?id=108949),
[Doom Eternal 2020](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2020/RenderingDoomEternal.pdf).

## UTA-0322 — finer shadows where memory allows

- **One atlas; each light's tile sized by its screen coverage**, from fixed
  power-of-two sizes (Unity HDRP, Flax) [verified]. DOOM 2016 used an 8k
  by 8k atlas on PC [verified, wiki figure].
- **Cache each shadow map's static part** (DOOM 2016, Flax) [verified]: draw
  the BSP once, then each frame copy it and draw only what moves. A light
  with nothing moving in reach is not redrawn. Fits UT_Ants: the BSP is
  static.
- Budget redraws per frame, nearest first [memory]. Snap a directional
  light's projection to whole texels [memory].
- Virtual shadow maps are not worth it at this tier.

Links: [DOOM 2016 study](http://www.adriancourreges.com/blog/2016/09/09/doom-2016-graphics-study/),
[Flax shadows](https://docs.flaxengine.com/manual/graphics/lighting/shadows.html),
[Microsoft shadow techniques](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/dxtecharticles/common-techniques-to-improve-shadow-depth-maps).

## UTA-0292 — baked probes: bounce strength and sky light

- **Unity lifts bake albedo with a power curve, not a multiply [verified]:**
  `min(pow(albedo, 1 / boost), maxOutput)`. Dark texels lift more than
  bright ones, so a bright texture is not flattened to the cap. UTA-0292
  ships `min(4 × albedo, 0.9)` (the user's choice); this is the
  alternative if bright surfaces read washed out.
- **Boost only bounced light, never direct** — Unity's Albedo Boost and
  Unreal Lightmass's Diffuse Boost do the same [verified]. UTA-0292 does.
- No engine documents a fixed clamp; about 0.03 to 0.9 for non-metals is
  common guidance [memory].
- **Storage.** The ambient cube is Valve's Source format. ZH3 (Roughton et
  al., I3D 2024) fixes L1 spherical harmonics' artefacts for a few
  instructions [verified]; the upgrade if the cube shows banding between
  axes.
- **Leak fixes [memory]:** offset the lookup along the normal and toward
  the viewer; drop probes inside solid BSP.
- **Sky occlusion** as its own probe channel would let a later sky change
  (UTA-0337) relight without a re-bake.

Links: [Unity Albedo Boost](https://docs.unity3d.com/2020.1/Documentation/Manual/GI-Enlighten.html),
[ZH3](https://www.activision.com/cdn/research/ZH3-Publication.pdf),
[DDGI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.10796),
[Valve 2006](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2006/SIGGRAPH06_Course_ShadingInValvesSourceEngine.pdf).

## UTA-0055 — banners, flags and water in the vertex shader

[memory, standard practice]

- **Flags:** two or three summed sines along the cloth, weighted to zero at
  the pinned edge, with a per-object phase (Crysis, GPU Gems 3 ch. 16).
  Take the normal from the analytic derivative.
- **Water:** three or four Gerstner waves (GPU Gems 1 ch. 1), steepness kept
  low so crests do not loop; fade the amplitude to zero at the shore.
- **BSP has few vertices:** subdivide water and banner surfaces into a grid
  at bake time, not with tessellation shaders; weld seams so neighbours do
  not crack. Keep shadows and collision on the flat surface.

Links: [GPU Gems 3 ch. 16](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/GPUGems3/gpugems3_ch16.html),
[GPU Gems 1 ch. 1](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models).

## UTA-0319 — UT99's WaterTexture and one stir factor per liquid

**Facts [verified]:** WaterTexture extends FractalTexture; WaveTexture and
WetTexture extend it. Each drop has `Type`, `Depth`, `X`, `Y`, `ByteA` to
`ByteD`. The surface rests at 128. "WaveAmp scales the amplitude of the
waves up or down at rendering time." Drop types: PhaseSpot (A frequency, B
phase); ShallowSpot (as PhaseSpot, half as deep); HalfAmpl (moves only
below 128); FixedDepth (A depth); Whirly and BigWhirly (A speed, B depth);
RainDrops (A depth, B radius); LeakyTap and DrippyTap; line drops at depth
128 act as barriers; AreaClamp damps.

**Suggested [memory]:** a stir factor summing a weight per drop type —
driving types about 1, ShallowSpot and HalfAmpl 0.5, damping types 0 — over
the texture's area. ByteA sets speed rather than strength. UTA-0319's paired
captures already ruled WaveAmp out as the strength, so fit the weights
against the original's frames.

**Gap:** the height-field update itself is closed C++; no public source
gives its damping.

Links: [Fire.WaterTexture](https://project-archives1.etc.cmu.edu/2001/fall/coyote210/Docs/undox/Fire.WaterTexture.html),
[Animating Real-Time Textures](https://unrealarchive.org/unreal-tournament/documents/reference/unrealed/unreal-animating-real-time-textures-manual/index.html).

## UTA-0258 — rows of lamps as one line light

- **Diffuse [verified, Karis 2013 eq. 16]:** the clamped line irradiance
  `2·saturate(n·L0/(2|L0|) + n·L1/(2|L1|)) / (|L0||L1| + L0·L1 + 2)`, which
  is a point light at zero length.
- **Specular [verified]:** Karis's representative point on the segment,
  with `α′ = saturate(α + length / (3·distance))`.
- **Shadows [memory]:** one shadow from the segment is the cheap standard
  while the row is short against the distance to what it shadows. Split a
  long row into pieces about as long as that distance, or where it turns,
  crosses a zone or meets an occluder. UTA-0258's open doubt — lit from the
  nearest point, shadowed from the midpoint — is the artefact this bounds.

Link: [Karis 2013](https://cgvr.informatik.uni-bremen.de/teaching/cg_literatur/Brian%20Karis%20-%20Real%20Shading%20in%20Unreal%20Engine%204,%202013%20Siggraph%20Course.pdf).

## UTA-0337 — a living sky, and outdoor maps without the box look

- **Moving clouds [memory]:** keep the captured sky cube; add one or two
  cloud layers on a virtual dome (`uv = dir.xz / dir.y`, faded to the
  horizon), scrolled at two speeds for parallax. A flowmap adds swirl. One
  or two texture reads per sky pixel.
- **Cloudy or clear [memory]:** judge the cube's upper half at bake time —
  pale, varied texels are cloudy, an even blue gradient is clear — with a
  per-map override for painted or space skies.
- **Sun:** an analytic disc and halo. Take its direction from the cube's
  brightest blob, or the sky zone's brightest light, or a per-map table.
  **Bake the same direction as a directional light**, so the light on the
  ground agrees with the sun in the sky.
- **Atmosphere:** Hillaire 2020 scales from mobile to PC with small lookup
  tables [verified]. For UT_Ants take only its aerial-perspective idea: a
  distance haze tinted by the sky's horizon colour, not a replacement sky.
- **Dissolving the box:** height fog and distance haze whose colour is
  sampled from the sky cube along the view, so far walls fade into the
  sky's horizon rather than a flat fog colour; blend where a wall meets a
  sky surface.
- **Risks:** a cloudy/clear guess misfires on stylised skies; a sun that
  disagrees with the baked light contradicts its shadows; moving clouds
  over light baked once should cast no cloud shadows, or only on the sky's
  share of the light.
- Nothing documented was found on how OldUnreal 469 or the Quake ports
  treat skies.

Links: [Hillaire 2020](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14050),
[Hosek-Wilkie](https://cgg.mff.cuni.cz/projects/SkylightModelling/),
[Unreal Wiki: SkyBox](https://unrealarchive.org/wikis/unreal-wiki/Legacy:SkyBox.html),
